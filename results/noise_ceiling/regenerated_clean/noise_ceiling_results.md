# Results — delay-period noise ceiling

On the corrected delay betas, region-level noise ceilings were modest and differed across
subjects. Subject-level means were 0.28 (sub-01), 0.43 (sub-02), 0.27 (sub-03), 0.22 (sub-05),
and 0.53 (sub-06). The subject-balanced mean was 0.35 (SD of the five subject means, 0.13).
Because each subject has 360 regions, the pooled mean across regions is the same 0.35; the pooled
SD is 0.28. Medians ranged from 0.00 (sub-05) to 0.57 (sub-06). The proportion of regions with a
noise ceiling of exactly 0 was 0.05 in sub-06, about 0.30 in sub-01 and sub-03, and 0.57 in
sub-05. The proportion above 0.5 ranged from 0.23 to 0.62. About 12–55% of the raw split-half
correlations were negative, depending on the subject.

Network-level estimates were more variable across subjects. Subject-level means were 0.30, 0.54,
0.13, 0.00, and 0.65. The subject-balanced mean was 0.32 (SD 0.27). The pooled mean was also
0.32 (pooled SD 0.32); 22 of 60 subject-by-network values were 0. In sub-05 every network had a
non-positive mean split-half correlation, so every network noise ceiling was 0. In sub-06, 10 of 12 networks exceeded 0.5. The sub-03 network mean was 0.13, with 6 of 12 networks at 0. Subject means should be read separately from the group mean.

These values replace the previous report of a region mean of 0.657 (SD 0.298) and a network mean
of 0.711 (SD 0.234). Those figures were the pooled means of the earlier maps, which were built
from mis-indexed and duplicated delay vectors and from a per-iteration Spearman–Brown correction.
Scoring those same contaminated inputs with the present estimator still produced subject-balanced
means of 0.69 (regions) and 0.71 (networks). The clean inputs do not reproduce those numbers.
Re-introducing only the duplicate-storage pattern in sub-01 raised that subject’s region mean
from 0.28 to 0.61 and the network mean from 0.30 to 0.59.

The group conclusion does not depend on Fisher versus arithmetic or median aggregation of the
positive split-half correlations: those alternatives moved subject means by only a few
hundredths. It does depend on refusing the per-iteration correction. That diagnostic was negative
on average in several subjects and exceeded 1 in absolute value in 185 of sub-05’s regions.
Permuting the 12 condition labels in one half reduced subject-balanced means to about 0.03
(regions) and 0.04 (networks). Applying the same permutation to both halves left the estimates
unchanged. Equalizing trial counts across variants lowered the subject-balanced means to 0.22
(regions) and 0.19 (networks), so the primary result is not produced by the variants with the
most repetitions, but reliability is higher when those repetitions are kept. Excluding records
whose 95th percentile absolute PSC exceeded 100 raised the subject-balanced network mean from
0.32 to 0.42 and gave sub-05 a network mean of 0.33; the primary maps do not apply that
exclusion.

The corrected maps therefore show reproducible task-variant structure in some subjects and in a
subset of regions and networks, concentrated in sub-02 and sub-06. They do not show uniformly
high reliability, and they do not support a single noise ceiling as an upper bound for
computational models.
