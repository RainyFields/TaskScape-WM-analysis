# Proposed Results text — spatial-pattern similarity (Section 4.5 / Figure 8)

Numbers are from the regenerated `clean_verified` analysis (fixed GLM-column mapping, one record per physical delay beta, block-file-verified labels). Family values are means of the 12 task-level estimates after averaging the 50 split-half iterations. Split-to-split SDs are Monte Carlo variability of the partition, not sampling error across participants.

---

Within-subject spatial similarity generally exceeded across-subject similarity, although its magnitude varied across task families and spatial resolutions (Figure 8). At the vertex level, mean within- / across-subject correlations were 0.84 / 0.03 (DMS), 0.17 / 0.01 (1-Back), 0.21 / 0.02 (InterDMS) and 0.10 / 0.02 (CTXDM). Parcel-level values were 0.89 / 0.01, 0.28 / 0.04, 0.33 / 0.05 and 0.20 / 0.04. Network-level values were 0.92 / 0.00, 0.40 / 0.22, 0.47 / 0.18 and 0.23 / 0.15. Monte Carlo SDs of the task-level means across the 50 splits were small relative to between-participant and between-pair spread (typically 0.02–0.15 for within-subject network estimates; ≤0.01 for most vertex-level across-subject means).

DMS was the only family with uniformly high within-subject reliability at every resolution (subject range at the network level 0.78–0.99). Within-subject similarity for 1-Back, InterDMS and CTXDM was moderate at the network level and substantially lower at the vertex and parcel levels, with large differences among participants (for example, network 1-Back location ranged from 0.24 in sub-05 to 0.85 in sub-06). Across-subject similarity was near zero at the vertex and parcel levels for all families. At the network level it remained low for DMS (mean 0.00; pair range −0.55 to 0.88) but was clearly non-zero for 1-Back (0.22), InterDMS (0.18) and CTXDM (0.15), with substantial pair heterogeneity (network 1-Back location pair range 0.03–0.74). Low group-average correlation therefore cannot be read as evidence that every pair is dissimilar, nor as proof of idiosyncratic cognitive strategies.

Among the 12 task variants, network-level within-subject means ranged from 0.17 (CTXDM CIL) and 0.26 (InterDMS identity ABBA) to 0.92 (DMS). Network-level across-subject means were highest for 1-Back location (0.36) and InterDMS location ABAB (0.38) and lowest for DMS (0.00) and InterDMS category ABAB (0.06).

A task-label derangement of one participant left matched-task across-subject similarity at the network level nearly unchanged for 1-Back (0.22 → 0.19), InterDMS (0.18 → 0.18) and CTXDM (0.15 → 0.18). Shared network structure in those families is therefore not demonstrated to be task-specific and is not interpreted as such. Spatially shuffling one participant drove that participant’s vertex-level across-subject pairs to approximately zero while leaving that participant’s vertex-level within-subject similarity unchanged. Cloning one participant into a second slot raised that pair’s similarity well above typical across-subject values. Pearson *r* was invariant to a positive affine transform of one participant’s maps (maximum |Δ*r*| = 7 × 10⁻⁷).

Four runs in sub-06 were relabelled using the matched stimulus block file. Replacing those labels with the original lookup table, or excluding runs whose behaviour fitted another rule, did not change the family-level pattern. Extreme percent-signal-change values were confined to a small number of vertices with near-zero PSC baselines and a small fraction of trials (0–1.1% of records per participant with cortical |β| 95th percentile > 100); they are treated as a sensitivity case rather than removed from the primary estimates.
